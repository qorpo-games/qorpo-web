AI-assisted cut-out textures: review the edge on light and dark

Q
QORPO Studio3 November 2026 · 5 min read

Review an AI-assisted cut-out texture over both light and dark backgrounds before approving it for a game. A clean-looking image on one background can hide a pale fringe, missing transparency or an edge that breaks at normal play size. Separate the colour image, its mask and the final material before changing several import settings at once.

Start with the intended use

A leaf decal, a soft particle and a hard-edged interface symbol do not necessarily need the same treatment. Write down what the player should see and where the image will appear. A beautiful isolated preview is not the whole acceptance condition.

This article proposes a bounded review exercise for a cut-out leaf graphic. It is not a claim about a particular generator's accuracy or a report of measured production savings. AI assistance changes how a candidate may be produced; the shipped material still needs to work in context.

For the broader asset handoff, see the texture import checklist. Here we focus specifically on the boundary between the visible shape and its background.

Check whether the exported file contains the expected information

Open the colour and transparency views separately in an image editor that can display them. Does the shape end where you expect? Is there a real alpha channel, rather than a checkerboard painted into the colour image? Does the exported file preserve the channel you inspected?

Epic's texture-mask documentation explains masks as grayscale information, including information stored in an individual texture channel. It also warns that alpha must be included during export when that channel is used.

That is the narrow sourced principle. The following inspection sequence is our editorial review proposal, rather than a claim that one specific compression option fits every texture or platform.

Use two backgrounds before the game scene

Place the same candidate over a light panel and a dark panel. Keep the image size identical. A pale border may be obvious against the dark panel while a dark outline may stand out against the light one.

Do not erase every soft pixel automatically. The intended graphic might include a feathered edge. First identify whether the transition is deliberate and whether it carries unwanted background colour. Record the visible symptom before deciding what to change.

For our imagined leaf, suppose a light halo appears only on the dark panel. Save that observation with the candidate version. It is more useful than saying that the asset looks vaguely wrong or asking for a more detailed generation without explaining the defect.

Then inspect the material the player will actually see

Put the graphic into the intended material and test scene. Compare a close inspection with the normal camera distance. Keep the background, scale and exposure consistent between versions.

A problem visible in the source file suggests a different investigation from one that appears only after import or material setup. The latter can involve the chosen channel, material behaviour or platform-specific texture handling. Do not assume the generator is responsible for every downstream difference.

Our mip review guide addresses detail at play distance. Use that check alongside edge review, rather than treating a sharp full-resolution source as proof that the distant result is correct.

Change one part of the chain

Suppose the source mask looks correct but the material shows a rectangular border. Check which channel is connected and what the material expects before editing the artwork. If the original already has an unwanted fringe, preserve an untouched copy and prepare a corrected candidate separately.

After changing one thing, repeat the same two-background and in-scene comparison. Record the file, material instance, import configuration, engine version and target platform. Do not mix a new export with unrelated lighting and camera changes and then attribute the improvement to one of them.

An AI-generated mesh review follows a comparable principle: test the specific player-facing boundary, rather than approving the asset only in an attractive standalone view.

Preserve authorship and usage information

Visual acceptance is only one part of a handoff. Keep the source, the tool or workflow used, the approved revision and the relevant usage permissions with the asset. Do not imply that passing an edge test establishes ownership or a generator's legal status.

When an artist changes the silhouette after generation, preserve that reviewed version. A later export from an earlier candidate can quietly reintroduce a fringe that the team already removed. A filename without version context is a weak handoff.

Questions about alpha review

Is a checkerboard preview proof of transparency?

No. Verify the actual channel data in the exported file. A checkerboard can also be part of an ordinary opaque picture.

Should every cut-out use one compression preset?

This article does not prescribe a universal preset. Check the texture's purpose, the material and the platform, then consult the documentation matching your engine version. Colour and mask data have different roles.

What is a useful acceptance note?

Describe the intended size and backgrounds, the reviewed revision and what was checked in the scene. Keep unresolved cases visible. “Reviewed on these backgrounds” is more defensible than “works everywhere.”

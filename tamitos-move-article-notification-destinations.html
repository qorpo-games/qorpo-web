TAMITOS Move article links: preparing a notification that opens the right page

Q
QORPO Studio19 November 2026 · 4 min read

The reviewed TAMITOS Move development snapshot prepares an article notification to open a specific destination for its language, while checking whether the installed app supports article taps. That is preparation in code and documentation. It is not evidence that a campaign was sent or that every installed app supports the interaction.

The distinction matters because writing a notification and delivering a useful reading experience are separate tasks. A short message can be correct while its destination, language or app behaviour still needs verification.

Start with the page the reader should reach

An article notification needs a clear purpose before it needs a catchy sentence. In this case, the reviewed preparation concerns an explainer of what Move offers. The intended reading destination is established in advance, rather than invented from whatever text appears in a message.

The public English Move comparison article is one of those destinations. The corresponding Slovak page has its own URL and content.

This is a small example of a wider studio principle: a link is part of the product interaction. Its usefulness depends on the page it reaches, not just on whether it is technically clickable.

Language should be honest about the destination

The inspected preparation defines Slovak, Czech and English notification copy. English leads to the English article. Slovak leads to the Slovak article. The Czech copy explicitly says that its destination article is Slovak.

That disclosure is useful. A Czech message should not imply a Czech article exists when the destination is actually another language. It gives the reader an accurate expectation before opening the page.

Our article language-pair verification guide covers published counterpart links. Notification routing adds another entry point to the same question: does the language promised to the reader match the actual page?

The installed app needs the right behaviour

A notification can reach a phone whose installed app predates support for the intended article interaction. The reviewed development work therefore distinguishes a compatible client from an older client.

The accompanying handoff document requires verification of actual app registration and article taps before rollout. A development flag or a code path alone cannot establish what happens on someone’s phone.

This article does not claim that compatibility was checked on a live device during publication. It describes the preparation visible in the inspected snapshot, including the explicit requirement to verify the app behaviour later.

Cold and background taps are different cases

A model acceptance review would open the article from a notification while the app is closed, then repeat with the app in the background. Those are proposed verification cases, not results recorded for this article.

In both cases, the reviewer should check the actual destination and the language of the page. If the app opens but leaves the reader on an unrelated screen, the interaction has not achieved its purpose.

Record the app version and starting state alongside the result. That makes a later report about a failed tap more useful than a general statement that links sometimes fail.

A prepared campaign is not a delivered message

The reviewed handoff explicitly says that deployment and device registration do not create or send this campaign. Preparation can exist without a campaign being queued.

The source tests use synthetic accounts and mocked transport. They express intended behaviour, including language destinations and handling of unsupported clients. Reading those tests establishes their intent; it does not mean they were executed successfully during this review.

Our prototype versus production guide explains why these boundaries matter across the studio. A development artifact and a verified user-facing release deserve different wording.

Acceptance does not mean reading

Another useful distinction concerns evidence after a send. A transport service accepting a message does not establish that a person opened it, read the article or understood the product.

The reviewed documentation keeps provider acceptance separate from a read receipt. Public reporting should preserve that distinction instead of turning a technical acknowledgement into engagement.

A model measurement plan could compare accepted notifications with confirmed article opens, using an agreed definition for each. Without actual measurement, there is no basis for inventing an open rate, conversion uplift or number of people informed.

What would make the interaction ready?

A focused release review would verify the intended public pages, the installed app versions, the language destinations and the cold/background tap cases. It would also confirm that the campaign remains a deliberate action under the normal notification preferences.

Those checks should lead to concrete evidence rather than a broad claim that every user will receive the message. Where a case is unverified, keep it unverified.

The reviewed snapshot is dated 23 October 2026. Its preparation is described here as a development example, not a newly announced rollout on 19 November. No campaign was created or sent while preparing this article.

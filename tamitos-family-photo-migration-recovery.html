TAMITOS update: restoring the original family photographs

Q
QORPO Studio28 October 2026 · 5 min read

The family photographs on TAMITOS Move were restored on 25 October 2026 after their public image URLs stopped loading. The repair returned the original files to the same locations, then checked that all seven story covers loaded on the public story list and the Slovak and English Move pages. It did not change donation records or replace families with generated images.

This is a small studio update with a practical lesson: a page can remain online while the images that give it meaning are unavailable. For a service built around real people, restoring those images is part of restoring the experience.

The visible problem came first

The report was straightforward. Visitors could read the family cards but could not see the photographs. The issue affected the people whose stories appeared on the TAMITOS Move page, rather than a decorative illustration elsewhere on the website.

The first check followed an image URL directly. That request returned a missing-page response. A second check against the current application origin showed the same failure. This mattered because changing a front-end layout or pointing the image at another public hostname would not restore missing source bytes.

The story list then confirmed the scope: seven cover images had completed their browser requests without decoding as images. The application had served its pages, but the photographs were unavailable.

Restore the original, not an approximation

The approved repair concerned fourteen existing JPEG objects, representing two size variants for seven covers. The original versions were recovered and restored under their existing keys. The restoration record includes byte-for-byte hash comparison with the recovered originals.

That is a different operation from selecting a similar portrait or generating a new person. A family photograph represents the family that supplied it. A visually plausible replacement would undermine that relationship even if it filled the empty rectangle.

The conceptual artwork accompanying this article illustrates media continuity. It deliberately contains no fabricated beneficiary portrait. The authentic photographs remain on the public family pages, where their context belongs.

Keep a media repair within its boundary

A missing photograph does not authorize rewriting a family's story, changing who receives a gift or adjusting a collection total. Those were separate systems and were left outside this repair.

The restored objects retained their original public paths. The work did not require changing family database records, donation history, authentication settings or DNS. Keeping the intervention narrow made the verification question clearer: do the same public pages now decode the same images?

This distinction also helps readers understand the product. TAMITOS combines a story world with practical support for families. The public image of a family, the description of a support need and a financial record have different responsibilities. Fixing one should not quietly change the others.

Check where people actually see the result

Object storage verification was one layer of evidence. Browser verification was another. A stored file can still be unreachable through the public route, served with the wrong response or referenced incorrectly by the page.

After restoration, all seven covers loaded on the public story list. The same seven loaded on the Slovak Move page and its English counterpart. The browser checks recorded successful decoding and nonzero image dimensions. Desktop screenshots preserved the visible result.

Those checks establish the repaired public image path. They are not a claim that every device was physically tested. Both size variants were restored, but a physical mobile viewport was not part of the recorded validation. A precise completion statement keeps that boundary visible.

A practical lesson for a migration

Here is an illustrative migration check, not a claim that every step was part of this incident. Before switching a media service, create an inventory of the public image paths used by important pages. Record the file identity, response type and expected visible context.

After the switch, check a representative page and the underlying files separately. Include a portrait, a landscape image, a small card size and a larger detail size. A homepage screenshot alone is unlikely to exercise all those cases.

Finally, retain a way to restore exact originals without broad changes to unrelated records. The useful recovery asset is not merely a directory called “backup.” It is a traceable mapping between the approved image, its public path and the page that depends on it.

What this update does and does not demonstrate

It demonstrates a completed media restoration with recorded public browser checks. It does not demonstrate a new donation feature, a larger sponsor fund or a change to how movement points become funded help.

That distinction is consistent with the studio's approach to production evidence. The proof should match the claim. A successful repository change alone would not have established that visitors could see a photograph again.

The earlier publishing-chain article covers article metadata and generated pages. This incident concerns a separate dependency: the original public media those pages request after loading.

Common questions

Were the family photographs generated again?

No. The repair restored exact original files. The new hero for this article is clearly labelled conceptual artwork and does not depict a real beneficiary.

Did this change donations?

The media repair did not alter donation records or the separation between movement support and direct gifts.

Where can I see the restored pages?

Visit TAMITOS Move and follow the public family stories. For the broader product context, read how TAMITOS connects stories and support.

Small repairs deserve clear evidence too. In this case, completion meant that the original photographs were visible again at the URLs the pages already used.

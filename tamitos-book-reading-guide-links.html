TAMITOS book pages: useful reading guides beside the buying decision

Q
QORPO Studio23 November 2026 · 5 min read

The TAMITOS book pages now offer a short route from choosing a book to using it together. A reading-guide block links to three existing articles in each language: the difference between book 1 and book 2, reading without requiring answers, and choosing books for a home library. The links are checked during the website build rather than assembled from guessed article addresses.

This is a small studio website feature with a practical purpose. A family looking at a book may need help with the next decision, not another promise about what the story will achieve. The feature joins the product page to information already available elsewhere on the site.

Three questions beside the book

The block on the English page is called “Before you choose a book”. Its three destinations answer different questions. One explains the distinction between a published book and a preorder. Another offers ideas for reading with a child who does not want to answer questions. The third is a selection for a home library. The Slovak page links to the corresponding Slovak articles.

These are not three interchangeable purchase buttons. Someone who already knows which edition they want can continue towards the seller. Someone unsure how reading together might work can leave the product explanation, read a relevant article, and return with a more specific question.

The current TAMITOS book page contains the block in its FAQ area. It sits alongside existing information rather than replacing the book description. Our earlier explanation of the publishing chain describes why connecting a story to a reliable live page involves more than preparing the text.

Why the build checks each destination

The implementation reads the already-built local article files before it updates either language page. A destination must exist, contain a nonempty main heading and not carry the checked noindex metadata. If one of these checks fails, the operation stops.

This is a deliberately bounded check. It does not prove that an article is factually complete, that every link inside it works, or that a search engine has indexed it. It verifies that these particular destinations satisfy the build's basic publication conditions before the book pages point to them.

That distinction is useful for a small publishing workflow. A missing article should produce a visible build error, rather than silently becoming a broken recommendation on a product page. Editorial review and a live release check still have separate jobs.

Prepare both languages before writing either

The script prepares the transformed Slovak and English pages before writing either result. A problem found while preparing the second language therefore does not leave the first language newly updated on disk.

This matters when a pair of pages belongs to the same release. A reader should not receive a completed navigation feature merely because their language happened to be processed first. The approach also makes failures easier to inspect: the release can retain its previous consistent pair until the missing destination is addressed.

This is a build safeguard, not a claim about every possible failure in deployment. A website still needs its normal release process and production verification. Our article on paired article URLs covers the separate problem of identifying the correct language counterpart.

Keep a repeatable transformation

The transformation removes its previous guide block and style block before inserting the current versions. Repeating it is intended to leave one set of links, not add another copy at every build.

The accompanying tests inspect this repeatability and check that existing headings, structured data, FAQ details, images and seller links survive the transformation. They also include missing-target and unpublished-target cases. Those checks describe what the test suite is designed to protect; they are not evidence that every part of the book page has received a complete usability audit.

A useful review can therefore ask two concrete questions: is the intended block present once, and are the surrounding decisions still available? Counting links alone would miss a feature that displaced an important existing section.

A model release review

Consider a hypothetical edit that changes the English reading article's slug. Before release, update the intended destination and confirm the built file exists. Then build both book pages. Inspect the three visible link labels and open their actual destinations.

Next, compare the product page before and after the change. The existing seller links, book artwork and FAQ content should remain available. Check the smaller screen too: a helpful reading link becomes less helpful if its label is difficult to follow or its focus indicator disappears.

This is a proposed review example, not a claim that such a broken release occurred. It illustrates why navigation needs both a source check and a browser check.

What the links do not promise

The guide block does not turn a story into a treatment or guarantee a response from a child. Nor does an article about editions establish today's price, shipping availability or delivery date. Readers should check the selected seller and edition before ordering.

TAMITOS is our own studio product, and these are our own editorial guides. Their value should come from the clarity of the questions they answer. Explore TAMITOS within the studio, then use the book page to choose the information relevant to your family.

AneeMate Brand Kit: Match the Cover to the Gameplay

Q
QORPO Studio28 November 2026 · 5 min read

AneeMate's brand kit can help a creator package a video, but a cover image does not establish what happened in that video. Choose the official asset for the publishing format, keep the logo intact, and check every gameplay claim against the footage viewers can actually watch.

The public AneeMate brand kit brings logos, covers, thumbnails, story templates, clips and a creator brief into one place. Checked on 28 November 2026, it is a useful starting point for presentation. This article explains how to use that material accurately, rather than announcing a new game feature or a new creator payment arrangement.

Start with the video, then choose the cover

Write one sentence describing what the recording demonstrates. A useful sentence identifies an action and its visible result: a player opens a menu, changes a setting, or completes a recorded interaction. Avoid expanding that sentence into a promise about an entire game mode.

Then select the cover. A dramatic creature render can make an episode recognizable without being evidence of a particular encounter. If the artwork was composed for the article or video, identify it as promotional or concept artwork where viewers might otherwise mistake it for a screenshot.

This distinction matters most when the cover depicts a more elaborate scene than the recording. The solution is not a vague disclaimer hidden after several links. Make the title, image caption and opening explanation agree about what viewers will see.

Match the format without distorting the brand

The kit offers different shapes for different placements. Its listed formats include vertical 1080 by 1920 assets, square 1080 by 1080 assets, portrait 1080 by 1350 assets and 1920 by 1080 YouTube thumbnails. A channel banner is a separate placement, with different visible areas from a video thumbnail.

Use the intended template as a starting point and preview the actual publishing surface. A correctly sized image can still lose a face, a title or a logo when a platform crops it. Check the phone preview as well as the full canvas before publishing.

Keep the official logo's proportions and colours. The creator brief specifically warns against stretching, recolouring or redrawing it. If a layout does not fit, change the layout around the logo. Rebuilding the lettering in an approximate typeface weakens recognition and creates another asset to maintain.

A model review before publishing

Consider a fictional creator preparing a short exploration video. This example describes a proposed editing routine, not a submission we have reviewed.

  1. Select the clearest recorded sequence and note the game version visible or documented for that session.
  2. Write a title limited to the action that sequence supports.
  3. Choose a cover from the kit, leaving space for platform cropping.
  4. Check the finished export with sound off, then check the spoken introduction against the caption.
  5. Add the relevant Early Access and commercial relationship disclosures before publication.

Suppose the cover shows a creature beside a building, while the clip only shows movement through an environment. A caption promising a complete building tutorial would be unsupported. Keep the artwork if appropriate, but describe the video as exploration and explain the illustrative cover.

The same discipline applies to a cut made from several sessions. Do not let editing imply that separate interactions happened continuously if that continuity is material to the explanation.

Read the brief beyond the downloadable files

The public brief asks creators to use original gameplay, disclose Early Access and avoid fake features or leaks. It also rejects income, price and reward promises. These are practical editorial boundaries, not decorative text beneath a download button.

Official footage also has an author. Do not present an official clip or another creator's recording as your own gameplay. Distinguish a supplied promotional segment from your original recording in the edit and accompanying text.

Where content is sponsored or rewarded, use the required relationship disclosure. The brief refers to #AneeMatePartner or a platform's paid partnership label. Check the instructions relevant to the actual arrangement; downloading a brand kit does not itself establish that you are a partner.

Keep the account explanation separate

A video about creator participation can accidentally mix presentation assets, account access and gameplay into one promise. Explain each only when it is relevant to the recording. Our creator account and playing entry guide covers the distinction between those entry points.

For a development diary, keep the source of each image and clip with the working files. The same habit appears in our game asset source record guide. For a reported visual problem, a reproducible recording matters more than a polished cover; see capturing a visual game bug.

Questions creators ask

Can a cover use official artwork without being a gameplay screenshot?

Yes, but describe it accurately. A cover communicates the subject and brand. A screenshot or recording supports a claim about something visibly happening in the game. One cannot automatically substitute for the other.

Does the brand kit confirm creator earnings?

No. Downloadable promotional assets do not prove an individual payment agreement, audience result or future income. Discuss a specific arrangement only from its actual approved terms.

What should I check immediately before posting?

Preview the crop, read the title against the footage, confirm the logo remains intact and include the relevant disclosures. Recheck links after exporting, since a correct working document does not guarantee a correct published description.

Explore the official kit and follow QORPO's studio updates for product context. Keep the final promise small enough that the actual video can fulfil it.

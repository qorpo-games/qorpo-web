Checking Unreal Static Mesh LODs with a Repeatable Camera Route

Q
QORPO Studio28 November 2026 · 4 min read

To compare conventional static mesh LOD transitions in Unreal Engine, use the same camera route, field of view, viewport resolution and platform settings before and after the change. Watch the mesh as its screen coverage changes. A distance written in a review comment is not enough to reproduce what the player saw.

Epic's per-platform LOD screen size guide explains that transition settings use the mesh's size on screen and can have platform overrides. This article concerns conventional static mesh LOD review. It is not a general explanation of Nanite's rendering behaviour.

Screen size is the useful comparison

A mesh can occupy a different portion of the view even when the camera is the same distance away. A field of view change or a different framing changes what the reviewer sees. That is why “the object pops at ten metres” is an incomplete report.

Epic's Static Mesh Editor UI reference documents the viewport's current screen size information. Use the editor's relevant information alongside a controlled scene capture rather than substituting an approximate world-space rule.

Record the mesh asset, its LOD setup and the platform configuration being evaluated. If an override is active, identify it. Otherwise, two people can inspect the same asset and compare different effective transition settings without realizing it.

Build a short route that exposes the transition

Choose one representative camera and a path that approaches and leaves the mesh. Include a pause near the transition region, then move across it in both directions. A static screenshot can show a silhouette problem, but movement makes a sudden switch easier to identify.

A useful route also includes the viewing angle that matters to the player. A pipe viewed end-on can hide a change that becomes obvious from the side. Keep the angle repeatable and preserve the same object placement between comparisons.

Do not add unrelated camera smoothing or exposure changes during this pass. Those changes may be worth evaluating separately, but they make attribution harder. Our exposure comparison guide describes why matching presentation conditions matters when reviewing art.

A model industrial prop comparison

Imagine an industrial pump with a circular housing, small bolts and a connecting pipe. This is a proposed example, not a report of a measured One Life asset.

During the near section, inspect the housing outline and the pipe connection. As the camera moves away, note whether small details disappear gradually or produce a distracting change in the overall shape.

Then watch the material response. A geometric transition may coincide with a visible change in highlights or shading. Record what changes instead of immediately blaming triangle count. The cause needs inspection of the actual mesh, normals, materials and LOD setup.

Repeat the route after one focused adjustment. If the transition becomes less obvious, preserve the before and after captures and the exact settings. A polished still image alone cannot establish that the moving comparison improved.

Keep resolution behaviour under control

If dynamic resolution or another resolution-related setting changes between captures, note it. A comparison intended to isolate an LOD adjustment should not silently include a different image-quality condition.

This does not mean dynamic resolution must always be disabled in a shipping game. It means the controlled review and the representative runtime review answer different questions. First isolate the visual change, then evaluate the intended gameplay settings.

See our dynamic resolution comparison workflow for the separate question of maintaining comparable capture conditions. Do not convert a clean visual transition into an unsupported performance claim. A less noticeable switch does not by itself prove better frame time.

Write a report another person can reproduce

Include the asset, engine version, map or fixture, platform settings, camera field of view and capture resolution. Describe the start and end positions in a way the next reviewer can find, rather than relying on memory.

Attach a short clip that crosses the transition and identify the moment of concern. State whether the issue affects silhouette, shading, missing detail or another visible feature. Avoid a vague “LOD looks bad” label when a more precise observation is available.

The visual bug capture guide adds the context needed for a useful handoff. If a performance claim is part of the proposed fix, attach the relevant measurements separately and describe how they were collected.

Questions during a review

Should all meshes use the same transition distance?

No. Screen coverage, shape, camera and presentation needs differ. Treat a universal distance as a hypothesis to examine, not a rule established by this guide.

Can a platform override explain a different result?

Yes, it is one of the first settings to check. Confirm the effective configuration before concluding that an asset change failed to propagate.

Does forcing one LOD prove normal transitions work?

No. It can help isolate the appearance of an individual level, but the normal transition still needs a moving comparison under the intended settings.

Has this example been profiled in a released game?

No. The pump example is an editorial fixture proposal. No frame-time saving or shipping-game result is claimed.
